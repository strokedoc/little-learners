# Design rules for a 3-year-old's learning app

Grounded in the AAP / WHO guidance for ages 2–5 (≤1 hour/day of high-quality content, ideally with an
adult) and in the known "hooks" that make kids' apps sticky — which this app deliberately leaves out.
Every feature must pass all of these.

## 1. Time: short, finite, and it ends on its own
- **Session timer** (parent sets 10 / 15 / 20 min, default 15). When it runs out, the child finishes the
  current question, then a calm "All done — time to play!" screen appears.
- That rest screen **stays locked** (even after closing/reopening the app) until a parent unlocks it.
- Each game is a **short round of 5 questions** (3 for puzzles, which take longer), then stops. No autoplay, no "next game" button, no
  infinite mode. Going back to the menu needs a tap.

## 2. No hooks
- No points, stars, coins, streaks, levels, badges, unlocks, collections, or surprise prizes.
- Difficulty **adjusts itself quietly** (levels 1–3 per game: an easy round moves up, a hard one moves
  down). She never sees a level, score or "level up" — it only keeps the games hard enough to need thinking.
- No variable rewards (the slot-machine effect). A right answer always gets the same gentle reply.
- No characters that beg the child to stay or are "sad" when they leave.
- No notifications, no daily reminders.

## 3. Calm senses
- Soft colours, no flashing, no fast or shaking animation, no loud fanfare.
- Sound is a calm spoken voice only; can be muted by the parent.

## 4. Never punishing, never rushed
- No timers on questions, no buzzers, no "wrong" sounds. A miss gets a soft "try again" and the right
  answer gently highlights after two tries.

## 5. Made for small hands and pre-readers
- Huge tap targets (≥ 90 px), everything is spoken aloud, no reading needed.
- Content matched to **her** level (strong on colours, shapes and counting to ~12; learning the alphabet;
  loves drawing and puzzles): most games build the alphabet — tracing, letter hunt, letter puzzles, and
  I Spy where every object is an alphabet word. Colours & shapes are combined ("the blue star") and
  counting goes to 10, so they stretch her rather than bore her. Tracing is forgiving (any order or direction).
- **Colouring** (for travel; crayons on paper stay better at home): a picture for every letter A–Z (29 pages), letter pages, plus one
  blank page. The crayon stays inside the shape she started in (no tap-to-fill). Two pages per round, nothing
  is saved or collected, and a finished page ends with "Show a grown-up!". If time runs out mid-picture she
  gets a one-minute warning, then rest. No film characters (copyright, and the site is public).
- Wrong taps teach instead of scold: in I Spy, tapping the wrong object just names it.

## 6. Safe and private
- No ads, no purchases, no accounts, no links out, no analytics, no data leaves the device.
- Works **fully offline** once opened (made for planes and car rides).
- The child's name is not in the app (it's on a public website).

## 7. Parent in control
- Settings and unlocking sit behind a **parent gate** (two adult sums in a row, so lucky guessing rarely works).
- Each round ends with a **"go find it" prompt** (e.g. "Can you find something red near you?") to move
  the learning back into the real world and invite an adult to join.

## Tip for parents
On iPhone/iPad, turn on **Guided Access** (Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access, then triple-click
the side button inside the app) so the child can't swipe out to other apps.
